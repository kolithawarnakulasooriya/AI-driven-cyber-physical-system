AI-Driven Introduction to Cyber-Physical Systems

Kolitha Warnakulasooriya, Ph.D. — Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering

What is a Cyber-Physical System?

A cyber-physical system (CPS) is a mechanism controlled or monitored by computer-based algorithms that is tightly integrated with the Internet and its users.[cite:1]

Physical Side

Physical device or system interacting with its environment through sensors and actuators, evolving over time in the real world.[cite:1]

Cyber Side

Computation and communication components that run algorithms, exchange data over networks, and make decisions based on sensed information.[cite:1]

These components are deeply intertwined, operate on different spatial and temporal scales, and exhibit multiple behavioral modalities that change with context.[cite:1]

The Field of Cyber-Physical Systems

Core Concepts

  • Hybrid systems: mathematical abstractions combining discrete and continuous dynamics, whereas CPS are real-world engineered objects.[cite:1]
  • Embedded systems: computational units embedded in physical systems; every CPS contains at least one embedded system.[cite:1]
  • Real-time systems: must meet timing constraints when responding to external changes, especially in safety-critical control.[cite:1]

Related Disciplines

  • Reliability engineering and fault tolerance.[cite:1]
  • Multi-agent systems and coordination.[cite:1]
  • Mechatronics and control theory.[cite:1]
  • Robotics and autonomous systems.[cite:1]
  • Internet of Things (IoT) and networked sensing.[cite:1]

Sensors of a Modern Car

A car is a rich example of a cyber-physical system, integrating dozens of sensors to monitor engine health, vehicle dynamics, safety, and comfort.[cite:1]

Powertrain & Environment

  • Intake air temperature and pressure sensors.[cite:1]
  • Coolant and exhaust gas temperature sensors.[cite:1]
  • Knock and oxygen sensors for combustion quality.[cite:1]
  • Common rail and brake booster pressure sensors.[cite:1]

Safety & Comfort

  • Reversing camera CCD and parking radar sensors.[cite:1]
  • Wheel speed and tire pressure sensors.[cite:1]
  • Crash and trunk lid sensors.[cite:1]
  • Air conditioning and gasoline particulate filter pressure sensors.[cite:1]

Together, these sensors feed the car's electronic control units, which continuously adjust actuation to ensure efficiency, safety, and user experience.[cite:1]

A System-Level View of CPS

We can model a CPS as a closed-loop system connecting the physical environment with embedded computation through sensing, communication, and actuation.[cite:1]

📡

Sensors

Measure physical variables from the environment or plant and convert them to digital signals.[cite:1]

🧠

Controller

Embedded code processes sensor data, runs control or AI algorithms, and decides actuation commands.[cite:1]

⚙️

Actuators

Apply forces or change physical states in the plant, closing the loop with the environment.[cite:1]

A communication network ties these elements together, enabling distributed controllers and remote monitoring in large-scale CPS.[cite:1]

Example CPS: Drone Swarms

Large-scale drone shows, such as those recognized by Guinness World Records, illustrate CPS at the intersection of robotics, communication, and coordinated control.[cite:1]

System Characteristics

  • Hundreds to thousands of drones acting as agents in a multi-agent CPS.[cite:1]
  • Tight synchronization of trajectories using real-time communication and control.[cite:1]
  • Safety constraints to avoid collisions and maintain formations.[cite:1]

Teaching Focus

  • Use drone shows to motivate CPS concepts like coordination and timing.[cite:1]
  • Discuss how AI can optimize patterns, trajectories, and energy use.[cite:1]

The Role of AI in CPS

Artificial intelligence extends CPS from manually tuned systems to autonomous and semi-autonomous systems capable of learning and adaptation.[cite:1]

Levels of Autonomy

  • Autonomous CPS: operate without human operators in many scenarios.[cite:1]
  • Semi-autonomous CPS: use autonomy under specific conditions but rely on human oversight otherwise.[cite:1]

AI at the Edge and in the Cloud

  • Edge AI on local controllers enables fast, low-latency decision-making.[cite:1]
  • Centralized AI hubs can perform predictive maintenance and global optimization.[cite:1]
  • Combined, they allow CPS to predict failures, optimize performance, and operate with high autonomy.[cite:1]

Autonomous Cars as CPS

Autonomous and advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS) rely on a rich suite of sensors to perceive the environment and support safe driving.[cite:1]

Typical ADAS Sensors

  • Ultrasonic sensors for parking assist and close-range obstacle detection.[cite:1]
  • Short- and long-range RADAR for obstacle detection, ACC stop & go, and reverse assistance.[cite:1]
  • Front cameras for vehicle detection, collision avoidance, and sign reading.[cite:1]
  • Side and interior cameras for blind-spot monitoring, lane change assist, and driver behavior monitoring.[cite:1]
  • LIDAR for precise distance and speed measurement and obstacle detection.[cite:1]
  • Infrared cameras for pedestrian detection and night vision.[cite:1]

Teaching Angles

  • Map each sensor to a CPS component (physical, cyber, communication).[cite:1]
  • Discuss sensor fusion and perception as AI-enabled CPS functions.[cite:1]
  • Relate ADAS features to control loops and real-time constraints.[cite:1]

CPS in Popular Culture: Knight Rider

TV series like Knight Rider (1980, 2000, 2008) anticipated AI-driven cars that communicate, reason, and act autonomously.[cite:1]

You can use clips from these series to spark discussion on how fiction foreshadows real cyber-physical systems and the ethical questions they raise.[cite:1]

Challenges and Ethical Considerations

Safety & Reliability

Because CPS involve physical motion and energy, failures or unpredictable AI behavior can cause real-world harm, demanding rigorous testing and formal verification.[cite:1]

Security & Latency

Networked CPS are vulnerable to cyber-attacks, and high latency can make remote control or cloud AI unsafe, motivating edge processing.[cite:1]

Accountability

When autonomous systems make flawed decisions that cause damage, assigning responsibility becomes complex, underscoring the need for transparent, explainable AI.[cite:1]

Putting It Together: Teaching CPS with AI

  • Start with intuitive definitions of CPS and everyday examples like cars.[cite:1]
  • Introduce the CPS architecture of sensors, controllers, actuators, and networks.[cite:1]
  • Use engaging case studies such as drone shows and autonomous vehicles.[cite:1]
  • Highlight how AI drives autonomy, prediction, and optimization in CPS.[cite:1]
  • Close with discussion on safety, security, and ethics to connect technology with society.[cite:1]
